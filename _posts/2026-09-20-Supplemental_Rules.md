# Supplemental Rules

## More Actions

This variant rule is not intended to change the way 5e's actions work in general, but rather add references to tabletop jargon that otherwise doesn't mean anything within 5e for use in homebrew.

While the way actions are described in Fifth Edition work perfectly well for the ideas present in the base game, when it comes to homebrew content it can feel somewhat limiting. The following are various actions including and extending upon those in the base game. An action may be more than one kind of action, such as a player taking a lengthy, distracting, or rooting action (though this specific instance would be better summarized as a full turn action, or a rooting sub-action).

## Base Actions

The following is a list of every kind of action creatures can take, including expansions on implied or commonly homebrewed rules.

### Action

Every creature has 1 action each round, which can be used to do any of the following, or any bonus action or free action, during their turn.

- Attack action
- Cast a spell
- Dash action
- Disengage action
- Dodge action
- Help action
- Hide action
- Ready action
- Search action
- Use an object action

### Bonus Action

Every creature has 1 bonus action each round, which can be used to do any of the following, or any free action, during their turn.

- Make an offhand weapon attack
- Cast a spell with a casting time of 1 bonus action

### Reaction

Every creature has 1 reaction each round, which can be used to do any of the following during another creature's turn.

- Initiate a readied action
- Make an opportunity attack
- Cast a spell with a casting time of 1 reaction

### Free Action

Every creature has 1 free action each round, which can be used to do any of the following during their turn.

- Interact with an object

### Full Turn Action

A creature can take a full turn action at the beginning of their turn. If they take a full turn action, they cannot take any form of action or move until the beginning of their next turn.

### Distracting Action

A creature that takes a distracting action cannot make an action or reaction on the same round. Distracting actions are made as part of your turn.

### Lengthy Action

A creature that takes a lengthy action cannot make an action or bonus action on the same turn. Lengthy actions are made as part of your turn.

### Tiring Action

A creature that takes a tiring action cannot move more than half their movement speed across their entire turn, and cannot take an action on the same turn. Tiring actions are made as part of your turn. This kind of action effect can be applied to other forms of actions (for example, tiring bonus action, tiring reaction, or tiring free action).

### Rooting Action

A creature that takes a rooting action cannot move across their entire turn, and cannot take an action on the same turn. Rooting actions are made as part of your turn. This kind of action effect can be applied to other forms of actions (for example, rooting bonus action, rooting reaction, or rooting free action).

## Improved Durability

This rule is intended to provide a boost to the character's durability and endurance considering the nature of the Jujutsu Kaisen Supplement reflects huge damage numbers and significant bonuses that can occur every turn within a combat encounter. This optional rule may mitigate that issue by improving characters' hit points and their maximums.

Starting at 1st level when a player selects either the Jujutsu Sorcerer or Heavenly Restriction classes, they gain an additional hit die to their hit die total. This additional hit die will provide extra hit points equal to the die's maximum rolled result + your Constitution modifier. For every level you gain as a Jujutsu Sorcerer or Heavenly Restriction, you gain an additional hit die towards your hit die total. The additional hit die then provides the same benefit as if you gain a level in your class.

For example, a Jujutsu Sorcerer that benefits from this rule will begin with 2 hit dice as opposed to 1 at 1st level. Their starting hit points are increased by the maximum rolled result of their hit die (d8 = 8) + their Constitution modifier. When this Jujutsu Sorcerer reaches 2nd level, they gain an additional hit die to their total and their hit point maximum increases by their Hit Points at Higher Levels found in the Hit Point section within the class description. If they had, say, Strong Body or Otherworldly Body, that increase would be applied as well.

## Summon Commanding Rules

Many sorcerers and curses have the capability to summon familiars or other types of creatures to help them in battle, such as shikigami or cursed spirits. The following rules describe how playing with these extra creatures works.

Your summons cannot use actions on their own. You can spend a bonus action to control the summon with a Simple Command, or with an action to order them a Complex Command. Whichever command you use, you can command multiple summons at the same time, giving them different orders or the same, but you can only control a number of summons individually per round equal to half your Charisma modifier (or Constitution modifier if you are a Heavenly Restriction), while summons you did not control defend themselves to the best of their ability. You can de-summon any of your summons for no action required on your turn or as a reaction to them being attacked if they are in your sight range, as long as they normally need to be summoned or manifested in the first place. Creatures summoned by your summon count toward your summon control limit unless the summon specifies otherwise.

- **Simple Command.** You control the summon's actions for that turn.
- **Complex Command.** You command the summon to perform a specific objective or task, which it will try to accomplish with the best of its ability. This can be anything such as protect that creature, grab that, defend that area, and so on. The summon will continue to try to achieve this task on subsequent turns, without the need to be further controlled by you until it finishes it or otherwise becomes unable to continue it (such as if it dies or if it's trying to attack a creature it doesn't know the location of). If it becomes able to resume the task again, it will do so. While a summon is carrying out a Complex Command, it continues to count toward your summon control limit each round.
- **Reaction Command.** Your summons can take reactions out of their own will as long as you allow them, with no action cost on your part.

### Shikigami Maintenance

When using shikigami as your summons, your focus is crucial in keeping them summoned. You must maintain concentration to keep your shikigami summoned. You must make the saving throw for each shikigami you have summoned. Shikigami maintenance does not inhibit other concentration.

### Technique Shikigami

If you're commanding and using a shikigami created by a Cursed Technique and you fail a concentration saving throw for Shikigami Maintenance, you may spend 1 cursed energy at will (no action required) to succeed instead. You must spend 1 cursed energy for each shikigami you have summoned that you fail the concentration saving throw for; however, if the number of shikigami you have summoned is equal to or higher than half your proficiency bonus, rounded down, you may spend 1 cursed energy to succeed all failures at once.

## Additional Status Conditions

In Jujutsu Kaisen, sorcerers undergo many trials that are better represented using more varied conditions than what's available in base Dungeons & Dragons.

### Berserk

A berserk creature must use its whole turn each round to attack the nearest creature to it. If it has multiple possible targets, it attacks one at random. If a creature moves out of a berserk creature's reach, it provokes an opportunity attack.

- A berserk creature has disadvantage on Wisdom and Charisma ability checks and saving throws.
- A berserk creature automatically fails Intelligence ability checks and saving throws, and can't concentrate on features.
- A creature becomes immune to the charmed and frightened conditions while berserk, and they will instantly lose these conditions in case they receive the berserk condition.
- The condition ends prematurely if the berserk creature starts its turn with no creatures in sight or hearing range, or if a spell or feature is used that removes the charmed condition (such as Calm Emotions). Creatures that are immune to psychic damage or the charmed condition are also immune to the berserk condition.

A creature may receive a special type of Berserk called Bloodlusted. Creatures with this condition behave similarly, but the condition is instead focused on one individual creature and removed when the source of the condition is dead or unable to be detected.

### Bleeding

- The creature loses an amount of hit points equal to either the CR or level (whichever is higher) of the creature that inflicted this condition + 5 at the start of their turns. This damage ignores temporary hit points and damage reduction.
- The creature has disadvantage on Constitution saving throws.
- The affected creature or a different creature that is within reach may perform a DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check as an action. On a success, they stop the bleeding. On a failure, they don't stop the bleeding and the creature takes the bleeding damage again that turn.
- If the creature heals 15 minus their Constitution modifier (minimum of 10) hit points, the Bleeding condition is also removed.
- Constructs or creatures that don't bleed are immune to this condition.

### Burned

If a creature receives a superior burn degree, the previous burn degrees will also apply to them.

**Degree Effects**

- **First:** Sources of fire damage deal 1d6 more fire damage per level of Burned.
- **Second:** You receive a 1d6 penalty to Constitution checks and saving throws.
- **Third:** The penalty to Constitution rolls increases to 1d8.

Finishing a long rest reduces a creature's Burned level by 1, provided that the creature has also received basic treatment or used topical ointments to heal.

If the creature magically regains hit points, whenever they regain an amount equal to half of the damage that inflicted the condition, the condition is removed. If there is no damage associated with the condition being inflicted, the amount healed must be equal to your Constitution score. Spells or features similar to Lesser Restoration can also remove this condition.

Creatures with fire immunity are immune to this condition.

### Chilled

- The creature's speed is halved.
- The creature has disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws and ability checks.
- If the creature has a feature that allows them to use an action as a bonus action, such as the Dash action, they may only use it as an action.
- The condition is removed no matter its origin or duration if the affected creature spends 10 minutes in a warm location. Spells and features of similar or greater power than Lesser Restoration can be cast to also remove the condition.

Creatures with resistance or immunity to cold or the exhaustion condition are immune to this condition.

### Confused

- The creature has disadvantage on Intelligence and Wisdom saving throws and checks.
- The creature has disadvantage on attack rolls, and attack rolls have advantage against it.
- The creature cannot concentrate, and must stop concentrating on anything they were concentrating on.
- Spells and features that remove the charmed condition also remove Confused. Similarly, creatures that are immune to the charmed condition, psychic damage, or are Constructs, are immune to Confused.

### Frozen

On some occasions in your adventures you may become partially or fully encased in ice. In those situations, you gain the Frozen condition, which has two stages and their effects listed below. If you have the Partially Frozen condition on three or more limbs, you gain the Fully Frozen condition. While you are Frozen, you are also Chilled.

**Stages**

- **Partially Frozen:** The affected limb counts as if it was removed. For every hour that passes with the creature's limb frozen, they gain a level of exhaustion.
- **Fully Frozen:** Become petrified but still aware of their surroundings, take 1d12 cold damage at the beginning of their turns.

The creature can make a DC 12 Strength saving throw using their action, regardless of the limb counting as removed, to break up to two limbs out on a success. If the creature is Fully Frozen, it may attempt breaking out with a DC 16 Strength saving throw using their action, ignoring the petrified condition, and breaking out on a success. Creatures constructed at least partially of fire are immune to being Frozen. Creatures reduced to 0 Hit Points while Frozen are considered stable, but must roll Death Saving Throws as normal when broken free.

### Hallucinating

- The creature's perception checks and passive perception receive a -5 penalty.
- The creature starts encountering false objects and scenarios, treating them as if they were illusions made by the Major Image spell that only that creature can perceive. These scenarios can vary according to what caused the condition.
- Constructs cannot be affected by this condition.

### Shocked

- A shocked creature's speed is reduced by a number of feet equal to the caster's proficiency bonus times 10 (or 20 feet if no caster).
- If a shocked creature's Strength or Dexterity modifier contributes to their Armor Class, it will no longer contribute to their Armor Class while shocked, unless said modifier is negative.

## Multiple Reactions

The supplement utilizes multiple reactions quite frequently. Due to that, the ruling for how they are used needs to be addressed. You may only use a single reaction to react to any given trigger (for example, you may not activate Infinity multiple times to keep re-rolling when you are targeted by a single attack). If a reaction you take allows you to take another reaction by using it, such as turning on Infinity as a reaction giving you access to the reaction to stop attacks or Canon Variant Projection being activated as a reaction and being able to use reactions in it, it does not count toward this rule.

## Damage Reduction

Any feature or ability in this supplement that refers to a "Damage Reduction" or "Reducing Damage" will work in the following way.

You will reduce the total damage from a single given source, even if that source contains multiple damage types. For example, if you receive damage from a Cursed Strike that contains both bludgeoning damage and force damage, you would take the combined total of the damage from the strike, and then reduce it, before taking the damage.

## Cost Reduction

Any feature or ability in this supplement that reduces the cost of a feature that spends a resource such as Cursed Energy will work in the following way:

The order in which you apply reductions, such as a flat reduction of your proficiency bonus and a reduction that makes 1 cursed energy count as 2, is entirely up to you. Usually, if not always, it is best to first half the cost and then reduce it by the flat amount.

If the feature you are reducing has a variable cost, you must first determine how much cursed energy you are spending on the feature (up to your spending maximum, which is specified within the feature). Then, you may apply your reductions. For example:

A level 20 Sorcerer chooses to use their Cursed Strike feature. They choose to spend 6 cursed energy, adding 6 dice of damage. Firstly, they half the cost, as they possess a feature that makes 1 cursed energy count as 2, reducing it to 3. They then apply their level 11 class feature to further reduce the cost by 3 (half of their proficiency bonus) to a minimum of 1.

If a feature does not have a variable cost, you do not have to determine how much to spend. Instead, that is already decided for you. The rest remains the same. Please note that not all reductions apply to all features. Please read everything thoroughly.

## Additional Weapon Properties

The supplement utilizes additional properties for weapons, more specifically for firearms.

### Long Reload

Similar to the reload property, a limited number of shots can be made with a weapon that has the Long Reload property. A character must then reload it using an action, or may use their bonus action to reload a single shot.

### Spread

A weapon with the Spread property fires multiple projectiles in a single attack, but the recoil and dispersion reduce accuracy. On a hit, roll the weapon's Spread die to determine the number of projectiles fired. Each projectile requires a separate attack roll, with each consecutive shot after the first taking a -2 penalty to hit. Each projectile deals the weapon's normal damage on a hit. If you roll a natural 20 or score a critical hit, the critical effect only applies to the first projectile.

## Sorcerer Weapons

Sorcerers are trained to use a variety of weapons in order to fight and exorcise cursed spirits. Any Cursed Tool or Semi-Cursed Tool counts as a Sorcerer Weapon. A sorcerer may make attack and damage rolls with its Dexterity modifier instead of any other modifier they would normally use. If a sorcerer has a 16 Strength score or higher and is using Strength to make the attack, they may add their proficiency bonus to their damage rolls with Sorcerer Weapons.

## Sixth Sense

As naturally as one can see, sorcerers can sense cursed energy from a distance. The ease of sensing cursed energy will depend on the intensity of the cursed energy you are sensing. A sorcerer will give off cursed energy signatures in a radius in feet equal to 5 times their maximum cursed energy when actively using cursed energy. When you enter a creature's radius you will sense their cursed energy and know in what direction they are relative to you. The Natural Sorcerer feat multiplies this radius by 24. Additionally, if you possess the Cursed Partner feat, you add your spirit's Cursed Energy to your own for the purposes of calculating the radius.

If a creature is hiding, you must make a Wisdom (Perception) check against their Dexterity (Stealth) check to track them if you are within the radius. A creature with the Six Eyes feat automatically succeeds on this check.

The Cursed Energy Tracker Advanced Energy Usage will allow you to add your Charisma modifier to the Perception check, and make it with advantage. The Cursed Energy Concealer Advanced Energy Usage will allow you to act as if you have 1/8th the Cursed Energy reserves, and make the Dexterity (Stealth) check with advantage.

A creature with both levels in the Heavenly Restriction class and the Physically Gifted Heavenly Restriction feat is completely immune to being tracked this way.

Finally, sorcerers' senses can be overwhelmed by a large amount of cursed energy around them. If there are at least double your passive perception cursed energy remnants or signatures close to each other, you can't tell them apart from each other, only knowing that it's cursed energy and not any other details about it.

## Cursed Concentration

Within the supplement, concentration is a vital component to the usage of jujutsu, so much so that people have learned to use cursed energy to reinforce their capability to concentrate while in high-stakes battles or if they are attacked during the time they concentrate.

As a bonus action or reaction to being targeted by an attack while concentrating, you may spend 6 cursed energy to activate Cursed Concentration for 1 minute.

For the duration it is active, the DC for your concentration saving throws is now 10 or 1/4 of the damage you took, whichever is higher. Additionally, in order to be forced to make the concentration saving throw, the attack must have successfully reduced your actual hit points and not your temporary hit points.

## Momentum Damage

Strength is weight and speed. In sorcery battles, the power of momentum and velocity does not go unnoticed. Those fast enough can use their speed to charge up powerful but risky strikes.

In order to begin using Momentum Damage, you must have moved 75 feet in the same direction in the same turn. For every 20 feet after the initial 75 you move, your next melee weapon attack or unarmed strike you make immediately after moving in the same turn deals additional bludgeoning damage equal to your Strength modifier, or half of your Dexterity modifier. If you miss the attack, you lose all built up momentum. Additionally, if you moved 75 feet (after the necessary 75 feet) or more and land a successful momentum attack, the target must make a Strength saving throw. On a failure, they are knocked back 1/4 the number of feet you moved before hitting them in a direction of your choice. If the creature collides with an unsecured object or structure, then the creature and the unsecured object or structure receive 1d6 bludgeoning damage for every 10 feet the creature had traveled before colliding with the surface. If it was another creature, they instead both take damage equal to half of your damage roll (rounded down). On a success, they are not moved.

Your momentum can be used against you. If a creature successfully hits you while you're benefiting from this rule, and you had moved toward that creature immediately before their attack, any additional damage gained from this feature is instead added to their damage roll.

The bonus damage can only be applied a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus multiplied by your Strength modifier (or half of your Dexterity modifier) to the same attack. The damage, regardless of this limit, cannot exceed 150 damage.
